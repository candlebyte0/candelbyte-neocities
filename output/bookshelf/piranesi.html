the no spoilers review

a delightful novel about unraveling the mystery of the world the main character finds himself in: an infinitely large house with an ocean on the bottom floor and clouds on the top. the story is told as a series of journal entries he has written describing his situation and what happens to him and the only other living person in the house, nicknamed "The Other". the main character is very likable, and sometimes cute in his naivete. typical of Susana Clarke, it is a blast to read, and in fact i read the whole book in one night (my sleep schedule be damned). i was left with both a hunger for more information about the world she described and the knowledge that removing its mystery would also remove a lot of the charm. i highly recommend it if you're looking for something fun to read!

world and characters

the book is fun for the world and the unraveling of the mystery, but doesn't have the most compelling characters. The Beloved Child of the House himself is certainly likeable and fun, he made a good perspective to read through, but his emotions were never close to as interesting as the mystery he was unraveling. which was, of course, in part about what had happened to him! still, due to the magic of the house effectively turning him into a different person, it never felt like he was reckoning with his own past.

fictional academia

Susana Clarke is great at writing fiction for nonfiction readers. i cannot stress enough how much i wish i could read the entire bibliography of Laurence Arne-Sayles. this was also exactly my reaction to reading Jonathan Strange & Mr Norrell, i wanted to read all the books on practical and theoretical magic. before the book, a little epigraph quotes Arne-Sayles calling himself "an anamnesiologist", saying he "[studies] what has been forgotten". i don't think i've heard a word quite that magical in a while.
